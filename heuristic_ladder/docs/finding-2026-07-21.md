# Finding — what the heuristic ladder shows (clean tables only)

*Date: 2026-07-21. Aggregated from `scripts/plot_ladder_results.py` over the JSONL
results; metric is `mem1_table_summed_f1` (mean). Only cells where the budget
binds cleanly and retrieval did not collapse are used as headline evidence.
Noisy / saturated slices (B=256 Oracle break, N=8 recall collapse, B=1024
non-binding) are reported as caveats, not claims.*

## Robust findings

**1. Timing dominates — and it is the only universally positive rung.**
Moving from "compress only on overflow" (H0) to "compress proactively before the
budget binds" (H1) is the largest gap in every clean slice: +0.12 (pool
single-obj), +0.36 (pool N=2), +0.44 (2Wiki N=2), +0.21 (union). It is positive
and largest across pool/union/multi-obj and across both datasets. This is the
load-bearing result of the ladder.

**2. Selection (H1→H2) is small and inconsistent.**
Adding relevance-based victim selection on top of proactive timing gives
+0.054 / +0.109 in the two Hotpot slices but **0.000** on 2Wiki N=2 and
**−0.019** on union. The signal is real in Hotpot but not robust across
retrieval difficulty or dataset.

**3. H3 (summarize + anchors) does not beat H2.**
The H2→H3 gap is essentially flat: +0.019 / −0.050 / +0.041 / +0.005. Whatever
H3 buys by preserving summarized anchors, it gives back by information loss;
on net it is not a reliable improvement over plain eviction. This is the second
robust finding, and it is a *negative* one: the summarization design point, as
implemented, is not earning its keep.

**4. Oracle is a clean ceiling only when the budget binds but is not
pathological; headroom grows under harder retrieval and multi-objective load.**
At Pool Hotpot N=1 B=512 the H3→Oracle headroom is +0.011 — i.e. the ladder
nearly reaches the gold-informed ceiling. Under multi-objective load it opens
to +0.282 (Pool N=2) / +0.109 (2Wiki N=2), and under harder (union) retrieval
it opens from +0.011 to +0.102. So Oracle is most informative as a ceiling
exactly in the easy single-obj pool case; elsewhere it marks real remaining
headroom rather than a tight bound.

**5. Union (harder retrieval) preserves the decomposition direction.**
Same 100 Hotpot examples, pool → union: recall 0.57 → 0.54, timing gap stays
large (+0.121 → +0.214), selection stays near zero, H3 stays ≈ H2, and Oracle
headroom **widens** (+0.011 → +0.102). The pool corpus was not lying about the
*shape* of the ladder; union just lowers absolute recall and exposes more
headroom. This validates generalizing from pool to union.

## e5 sensitivity — revises finding #2 (selection is *not* weak under semantic scoring)

`--retrieval e5` swaps the ranking backend (and the H2/H3 selection scorer) from
BM25 to `intfloat/e5-base-v2` (Search-R1/MEM1-aligned). Run on the same three
slices at B=512. For N=1 the BM25 n=200 run was filtered down to the same 100
`example_id`s the e5 n=100 run used (both seed=0), so the comparison is
apples-to-apples.

The clean cross-backend number is the **selection gap (H1→H2) within each
backend**: H1 and H2 share the same retrieval backend, so H2−H1 measures *only*
the value of adding a selection scorer, and comparing that gap across backends
isolates scorer quality.

| Slice | BM25 selection (H2−H1) | e5 selection (H2−H1) |
|---|---|---|
| Pool Hotpot N=1 | +0.044 | +0.041 |
| Pool Hotpot N=2 | +0.109 | **+0.195** |
| Pool 2Wiki N=2 | **+0.000** | **+0.091** |

**Finding #2 is revised.** "Selection is weak" was a BM25 artifact. On 2Wiki —
where lexical selection contributed *literally nothing* (0.000) — semantic
selection adds **+0.091**; on Hotpot N=2 it nearly doubles (**+0.109 → +0.195**).
Only on the easy N=1 case is it flat, where there is little to select among.
So selection *does* earn its keep once it is semantic, which is the regime the
published learned methods actually operate in.

**Finding #3 (H3 ≈ H2) is strengthened, not weakened.** Under e5 the sel++
gap (H2→H3) is **negative in all three slices** (−0.115, −0.117, −0.071): when
selection is already good, summarizing instead of evicting actively hurts. The
negative summarization finding is robust to the scorer.

**Timing (#1) still holds under e5** — timing gap stays large and positive
(+0.216 / +0.424 / +0.337).

**Caveat on Oracle/headroom under e5.** Oracle drops on the N=2 slices under e5
(Hotpot 0.854→0.746; 2Wiki 0.664→0.551) and headroom shrinks. This is **not**
clean across backends: Oracle is "perfect selection *given retrieval*" and gold
labels are assigned by title-match at retrieval time, so changing the retriever
changes which paragraphs get gold. Oracle/headroom are only comparable *within* a
backend. The selection-gap result above is the trustworthy cross-backend claim.

**Implication for the thesis.** Semantic selection reopening the selection
arrow means MEM1 could plausibly earn its keep on selection after all — so the
in-harness MEM1 comparison is more worth running than before, and should run on
the multi-obj / 2Wiki slices where e5 selection shows real gains.

## Caveats (do not quote as headline)

- **B=256 is stress, not ceiling.** H3 *beats* Oracle (0.403 vs 0.308) because
  under a tight budget Oracle hard-deletes and often retrieves gold then keeps
  zero supporting paragraphs; H3 summarizes and answers earlier. Treat B=256
  as a pathology probe, not a rung the story rests on. Prefer B≥512.
- **N=8 collapses.** Recall falls to 0.05–0.14 and summed F1 exceeds 1 (sum over
  8 objectives); the rungs are saturated/noisy. N=8 is reported for
  completeness, not used as evidence.
- **B=1024 barely binds.** On union B=1024 the rungs bunch (H0 0.535 → Oracle
  0.567) because compression rarely triggers; uninformative for decomposition.

---

## One-paragraph takeaway

Across pool and union corpora, single- and multi-objective load, and both
HotpotQA and 2WikiMultihop, **when** to compress (H0→H1, timing) is the dominant
and robust design decision; **what** to evict (H1→H2, selection) helps modestly
under lexical scoring but **earns its keep under semantic (e5) scoring** —
especially on 2Wiki, where lexical selection added nothing and e5 selection
adds +0.091; **summarizing** instead of evicting (H2→H3) does not beat plain
eviction under BM25 and **actively hurts under e5**. The gold-informed Oracle
is a tight ceiling only in the easy single-objective pool regime and opens real
headroom under multi-objective load and harder retrieval — which is exactly
where a better compression policy would have room to matter, and where the
in-harness MEM1 comparison should be aimed.
